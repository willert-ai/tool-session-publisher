# Repository audit — session-publisher

2026-09-07 · Revision 4 · **Audit/handoff ready after independent review and refutation, including the owner-perspective amendment. Implementation remains proposed, unperformed.**

## Outcome and authority

Real work should arrive as a small selection of accurate, recognizably personal posts that the operator wants to publish. The system should remove remembering to draft, finding the story, reconstructing evidence, and repeated rewriting. The operator retains judgment about personal meaning, disclosure, and whether/when to publish.

**Recommendation:** retain the local engine, private files, terminal review, and operator-controlled publication. Make the operator's evidenced perspective the organizing principle of selection and drafting: what the work meant to them, the business or personal choice it involved, and what they learned. Then complete the evidence, feedback and publication-recording loop. Better wording, more checks or more supply cannot supply a missing point of view; operational repairs remain necessary to deliver it reliably.

Included: session-derived singles and the existing weekly arc capability; source fidelity, freshness, voice, repetition, privacy; review/edit/copy; manual publication or operator scheduling; confirmation, bounded follow-up, and failure recovery. The manual skill remains the fallback. Excluded: automatic publication or scheduling by this system, X APIs, engagement optimization, reply duties, new hosted infrastructure, mobile/web review, multi-platform work, new subscriptions, and new content intake.

Settled boundaries: notes and bodies stay private outside the public repository; local launchd and subscription-authenticated Claude remain the carrier; publication authority stays with the operator. The later 400–650-character, three/four-paragraph register supersedes the old specification's 280-character examples. The owner selected a cold-reader check and weekly storylining; neither is silently removed by this audit. Technical designs below are proposals, not runtime authority.

The original audit phase authorized **audit and local documentation only**, and performed no live batch, private-state mutation, service change, publication/scheduling, contact, ticket, commit or push. The owner subsequently authorized committing, pushing, merging this documentation to main and cleaning merged branches. That repository-integration grant does not authorize the proposed implementation, private-state migration, installation or runtime publication actions; those still need their own authority.

### Owner feedback: a better-written account can still miss the person

After the initial audit, the operator reported that current drafts have much better shape and wording, but still feel too technical, detached and insufficiently personal. The desired post connects the work to a person's business concerns, choices, story or insight. Preserve the writing improvements; the new evidence changes the leading editorial diagnosis.

The operator's explanation—that AI-authored session wrap-ups foreground the AI process's technical insights—is a plausible source-perspective hypothesis. The audit has not established that all wrap-ups lack human perspective, or that the underlying conversations would supply it. Nevertheless, an accurate account of a fix can omit why the person cared. Plain language and comprehension checks alone cannot close that gap, and adding generic emotion would fabricate it.

The first editorial operation must recover a supported human angle: an expressed aim or concern, a consequential choice/tradeoff, something the operator noticed or reconsidered, or an explicitly stated business/personal consequence. Technical detail can make that story concrete; it should not determine the story merely because it is richly documented. No mandatory drama, vulnerability, first-person formula or sentiment score is required. The operator decides whether the result feels like their experience and has human meaning.

Keep source roles distinct: technical event evidence establishes what happened; attributable operator statements/decisions establish their perspective; AI retrospective interpretation is a hypothesis, not testimony about the operator's feelings. Search the matched session's already authorized records for that perspective, recording speaker/authorship and evidence location. Existing user turns or decision notes may help **if available under the same access boundaries**; availability is unknown. Do not broaden access to unrelated conversations or start collecting new material by default. If only a technical account is available, mark the perspective as missing and skip that candidate rather than manufacture a personal lesson. A small optional clarification path is an owner choice below, not a hidden requirement for daily interviews.

## Workflow findings

References below name source functions and baseline line numbers so findings remain reproducible without publishing private evidence.

1. **The completion event is wrong.** `queue.py:1504–1559` approves, attempts clipboard transfer, then records `posted` and archives—even on clipboard failure or `--no-clipboard`. The operator still has to paste and publish. This implements SPEC D3 faithfully; the contract itself confuses handoff with result. Copied-but-forgotten items vanish from actionable review. Introduce distinct handoff and confirmed-publication states, retaining exact final bytes and copy retries. Do not add publication automation.

2. **The personal feedback loop is disconnected, and its storage has diverged.** `draft.py:414–435` reads persona, guide, optional insights, vocabulary, seeds and task, but no approved corpus. Manual Stage 5.5 does read it. `queue.py:1003–1005` replaces the configured corpus pathname; replacing a symlink replaces the link, not its target. The current ignored regular file contains the entire synced base plus the August 31 approval. That is consistent with first approval severing the link; no contemporaneous pre-approval link snapshot was found. Preserve both copies before reconciling, resolve the canonical external destination before writing, and verify byte identity. Feed a few current-register approved examples into ambient drafting; historical examples remain immutable but need not be selected. Select from the approved final text and its approval/register provenance directly, without depending on generated axis tags. Heavy-edit tags marked `retag: pending` remain historical metadata, not an eligibility veto or a requirement for operator curation. Later withdrawal must remove an example from eligibility without deleting history.

3. **Selection measures documentation richness, not whether there is a good personal story.** `mine.py:583–609,746–764` rewards numbers, detailed outcomes, tags, implementation and same-day commits, before reading the narrative. Two session references can describe the same incident. Model/gate rejections receive no attempt record (`draft.py:691–716`), while any ledgered seed is permanently excluded. Retain exact identity dedup; add a small evidence-backed shortlist that pairs a concrete incident with an attributable operator perspective, source date and recent-topic comparison, plus bounded attempt cooldown. Prioritize that human/business meaning over an impressive implementation detail; general business platitudes do not count as personal perspective. Do not respond by expanding the number of drafts or by excluding this project permanently.

4. **Comprehension, truth and personal voice are different checks.** Digit membership in `draft.py:264–275,598` cannot prove attribution, units, causality, quotations or a universal claim; it even includes the reference despite the prompt forbidding that reference as content. Same-day repo commits are enrichment, not session-level proof. Queue review omits even the source reference (`queue.py:1728–1755`), making factual checking hidden operator work. Retain private source pointers, hashes and brief claim-evidence anchors, and show them on demand. The owner-selected cold reader should see only the body and name unclear referents; a separate source-fidelity pass must see the claim anchors. Neither model verdict certifies personal voice or connection. A technically clear, fully factual draft can still fail the owner's updated requirement when it expresses only the AI process's lesson. Source fidelity must also test the attribution of motives, beliefs, emotions and personal/business conclusions; the cold reader is not a substitute for finding those in the source. The deterministic gates are also looser than the prompt says: outer cap 700; paragraphs two through six only for bodies ≥400, rather than guaranteed 400–650/three-four (`draft.py:238–250,562`). Align claims and checks with the agreed register.

5. **Two privacy paths fail open.** Unreadable/missing private terms return an empty denylist (`queue.py:297–306`). Copying an already-approved mutable file does not revalidate its current bytes (`1532–1547`), so external edits or newly forbidden terms can bypass the earlier approval check. Required production inputs must be readable before a model call; validate exact final bytes and current denylist before every copy, and invalidate approval after body changes. Keep neutral working directory, tool disabling, input redaction and output D6 checks: user hooks/rules still reach the CLI. No actual leak was found. Prompt fencing is useful hygiene, not authority to obey session text.

6. **Operational success is not value health.** The queue is full while production reminders often fail. `review_reminder.sh:318–333,639,706` asks for a four-hour dialog lifetime without an enclosing AppleEvent timeout. Six approximately two-minute `-1712` failures strongly support a competing timeout as the cause, but the diagnosis still needs an authorized UI smoke. Keep the existing scheduler/reminder, repair its delivery, and report distinct no-material, capacity, model-failure and no-useful-output conditions. A healthy empty day is legitimate; a week of failures/rejects or a stale unreviewed backlog needs one actionable summary, not daily reassurance or repeated nagging.

7. **Locks and atomic renames leave cross-component gaps.** `run.sh:356–379` can delete a newly acquired lock before its PID is written and explicitly proceeds if locking fails; cleanup can remove a successor's lock. Launchd serializes its own job, but supported manual invocations expose this race. Queue and corpus writers use shared temporary names and no shared transaction lock (`queue.py:659–715,948–1005`). A drafted ledger event before entry rename (`1216–1226`) can consume an invisible seed after a crash. These are reachable interleavings, not observed corruption. Use an OS-held tick lock and a separate short mutation lock shared by queue/corpus actions, unique staging files, idempotent events, and reconciliation. Do not hold the mutation lock during model calls, an editor, or an operator prompt.

8. **Timeout recovery and documentation overpromise.** `call_cli` has a 300-second default, no duration telemetry or retry; an arc shares one call (`draft.py:471–509,718–728`). The Sunday 14-day window becomes three days on Monday (`run.sh:337–338`): no ledger event means eligible for dedup, not guaranteed next-day reselection. Persist a bounded transient retry with its source snapshot/freshness deadline. Record duration and actual model before changing budgets. Direct-child timeout does not establish descendant termination; test owned process-group cleanup for manual and scheduled interruption. No orphan was observed. Early history reports a real-CLI arc fixture success, later prose says stub-only; what remains unproven is scheduled full-document arc completion, not every possible arc call.

Installed 07:30/07:45 schedules, skill links and engine template agree on current scripts and the 300-second default. Both jobs execute this mutable checkout. README's opening remains interactive-only, SKILL says the trigger is unwired, and AGENTS has stale acceptance/reminder claims. Update those under the later implementation; don't edit the old spec into pretending that proposed behavior already shipped. Pin or snapshot the runtime installation during that implementation so development branch switches cannot alter unattended behavior.

## Proposed complete mechanism

1. **Prepare a small shortlist from real sources.** Resolve candidate narratives, keep private evidence and source time, identify the incident and the supported human/business angle, distinguishing the operator's own aim, decision or insight from the AI's retrospective. Record the perspective evidence before drafting; compare with recent drafted, killed, approved and published topics. Prefer a distinct supported story; skip when the event or the perspective is missing. Use existing attributable session material only within authorized access; absence is a source gap, not permission to invent or interview by default. Use the existing daily wake and weekly source window. Cap new candidate supply at six/week and the queued/approved candidate backlog at three. Present at most three due cards per sitting, shared between those candidates and handoffs needing action now. Future scheduled or explicitly snoozed receipts remain tracked but do not consume those editorial slots until due. Separately cap outstanding receipts at ten: at that limit pause further supply and explain the next due action once; do not require early confirmation or false abandonment to free capacity. All due confirmations share the three-card sitting and total time limits. These are proposed caps, not an instruction to expire the live backlog now.
2. **Draft with evidenced voice and bounded checking.** Use the calibrated persona and a bounded set of eligible approved examples. Organize the body around the supported human angle and use technical detail only where it helps convey what happened and why it mattered. Avoid laundering the AI's discovery into the operator's memory or adding unsourced feelings, motivations, business effects or a generic inspirational ending. Verify claims against evidence separately from the cold reader's body-only comprehension check, then rerun deterministic/privacy gates on any revision. Allow at most one repair of a candidate. Every repair invalidates both factual and comprehension judgments; rerun those checks on the revised bytes before queueing. Zero output is valid. Start with a quota ceiling of four model calls per ambient tick and six per deep tick, including selection, perspective extraction, checks and repairs, plus a whole-tick time ceiling determined from measured calls. Never spend extra quota merely to fill the card cap; persist a private incomplete attempt when the ceiling prevents verification. For singles as well as arcs, store completed checks against exact body/source hashes and checker versions; the next eligible tick resumes missing checks rather than redrafting. A four-call single that ends with a repair therefore resumes both judgment checks later. Changed source, body or checker inputs invalidate affected cached results; no partially checked candidate enters operator review. T4 owns these checkpoints, with T1 storage and T2 shared quota enforcement.
3. **Keep weekly storylining without all-or-nothing delivery.** Select a coherent set before drafting, maintain optional arc context/order, and require each member to stand alone. Instrument the current batch path first. If it still misses the bounded budget, build members separately using the same short arc outline, preserving the intended connected set. Already validated members survive a sibling failure; the failed member has one fresh retry opportunity. A multi-day arc may fill across daily ticks under the same caps; no hidden unbounded retry loop.
4. **Make one local editorial decision sufficient.** A card shows body, source date, brief provenance access, current status and optional arc context. Accept-and-copy, edit-then-accept-and-copy, kill or defer; an optional kill reason can inform future selection, but must not create mandatory annotation work. Copy uses exactly the approved bytes. A routine card should need no rewrite; spending two minutes repairing it is a signal to reject/defer rather than perform an unpaid drafting session.
5. **Finish the manual handoff and record reality.** The operator pastes into the chosen destination, checks final text/account and posts or schedules. Local states distinguish `approved`, `handed_off`, `scheduled` (operator-attested), and `published` (operator-confirmed, preferably URL). A failed clipboard remains recoverable; recopy never approves or appends twice. A destination-side edit requires recording/reapproving the final text and rerunning the local gate before treating that text as the canonical approved version. Approval is not an authorization for the agent to click Post or Schedule.
6. **Close feedback and exceptions in the same surface.** Publication confirmation and outstanding handoffs appear in the next bounded review, not a second daily task. A stale handoff is shown once with recopy/confirm/defer/abandon choices and then snoozed until its explicit follow-up; it is never automatically called published. Completed receipts feed simple usefulness/effort summaries. Operator feedback about personal relevance/resonance must change future angle selection as well as wording. During the trial, record detached/too-technical versus personally meaningful feedback only when given in the existing verdict, without adding another required action. Do not label historical approval alone as proof of the new resonance requirement. Only approved, non-withdrawn final text with reliable approval/register provenance feeds voice; stale generated axis tags are ignored. This includes heavily edited approvals such as the existing captured correction, without requiring automatic retagging or recurring operator curation. Publication status remains separately measurable.

Shared private record contract: stable source identity plus document location/hash and source/observation dates; a few claim-evidence anchors; a supported human-angle statement with authorship/speaker evidence and an explicit missing-perspective outcome; recent-topic/incident key with explanation; frozen drafted body; approved-body hash/revision; gate/model version; attempt outcome/count/deadline and completed check hashes/versions; decision/edit metrics and any explicitly supplied perspective verdict with its applicable entry/angle; optional owner-authorized clarification and attribution; handoff time/destination class; operator-reported schedule time; publication confirmation time/optional URL. Keep this alongside the existing file queue and event ledger. Public logs contain counts and clamped codes only. Don't store full prompt or model stderr in routine logs. All consumers use the same state vocabulary and migration version.
